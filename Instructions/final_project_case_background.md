# Final Project — Case Background

A fictional retailer wants to test a temporary pop-up selling reusable bottles, lunch containers, and small everyday carry accessories. Before making a larger commitment, the retailer wants to learn whether people passing a site will engage with this offer.

**Your Decision:** Advise the pilot-program manager to authorize one affordable location and operating window for a four-week trial, or explain why the retailer should wait and what it should check next.

---

## What Management Cares About

* **Priorities:** The manager values opportunities to reach passersby, reasonably stable operations, and responsible use of a limited budget.
* **Flexibility:** No fixed scoring formula or preferred location is supplied. Explain your priorities, compare a credible affordable alternative, and state what evidence would change your recommendation.
* **Learning Objective:** The trial is intended to learn about engagement. Guaranteed sales are not a prerequisite for running a trial.
* **Waiting Option:** If you recommend waiting, identify a specific unresolved issue that prevents a responsible test and propose an actionable next check.

## What the Data Can Tell You

The course provides two related tables:
* **Candidate Options:** Describes the available choices and costs.
* **Weekly Activity:** Contains historical passage counts for each option and week.

### Measurement Definition
* A **passage event** is one crossing in either direction of a consistently defined counting line near a site during the specified operating window.
* **Repeat crossings count again.**
* Counting rules and frontage-access assumptions are uniform across sites.
* A complete weekly count covers all 20 scheduled hours.

### Data Boundaries
* Counts describe **potential exposure**.
* They **do not** measure unique people, customers, purchases, revenue, profit, or product demand.
* The retailer has not yet operated this pop-up in the supplied historical data.

## What Remains Uncertain

* **Missing Data:** Eight weekly observations are missing. A missing count is not zero activity; no cause of missingness is supplied.
* **Outliers:** Some observed weeks are unusually high. No explanation is supplied, and a high value is not automatically an error.
* **Seasonality:** Summer and early-fall activity may differ from foot traffic during the proposed October–November trial.
* **Unobserved Demand:** Customer interest, transactions, and actual pilot results have not been observed. Describe them as future evidence if you propose collecting them.

> Use the observations to compare options, explain variability and completeness, and test one consequential analytical choice. A recommendation need not change after the test. Explain what you learned.
