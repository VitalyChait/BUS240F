# Final Project — Start Here

Work with your assigned team to advise a fictional retailer on a limited trial. Recommend one affordable location-and-time option, or explain why the retailer should wait. Compare a credible alternative and explain what evidence would change your recommendation.

---

## What You Will Learn

Use visual evidence to support a decision, verify the numbers behind your views, and explain their limits. 

> **Important Data Note:**  
> The supplied data count **passage events**, including repeat crossings. They **do not** measure unique customers, purchases, or sales.

---

## Starting Sequence

1. **Understand the Choice:**  
   Read the case background and field definitions. Identify the budget, possible options, and what the trial should help the manager learn.
2. **Check the Data:**  
   Use the preconnected Tableau starter. Explain what one row means, check representative counts and values, and distinguish missing observations from zeros.
3. **Compare and Test:**  
   Compare a preferred option with an alternative. Test one reasonable analytical change and explain what happens to your conclusion.
4. **Recommend and Explain:**  
   Present the evidence, uncertainty, next step, and what would change your mind.

*(The case background, data dictionary, and starter will be added to this section. Use the released versions for your analysis.)*

## What Your Team Submits

Submit a three-file package:

1. **Packaged Tableau Workbook:** `.twbx` format.
2. **Two-Page Brief:** PDF including your verification notes (exactly two pages total).
3. **Static View Backup:** A PDF backup of decisive views, fully readable without Tableau.

*Note: A separate presentation slide deck is not required. Each team member must be able to explain the analysis.*

## The First Checkpoint

You do not need a finished dashboard or final recommendation at this stage.

* **Requirements:**
  * State the decision.
  * Define a metric and its included records.
  * Make a preliminary claim.
  * Show one readable view with units and important exclusions.
  * Identify a question you still need to resolve.

### Example Structure:
> *"We are deciding between **[option A]** and **[option B]**. Our measure is **[definition and included weeks]**. The view suggests **[bounded claim]**. We still need to check **[uncertainty]**."*

*(Use your own evidence; no particular option or result is required.)*

## Presentation and Individual Work

* **Team Structure:** Six teams of four students each.
* **Session Format (20-minute slot per team):**
  * **6 minutes:** Presentation
  * **12 minutes:** Questions (each student receives 3 protected minutes of questioning)

  * **1 minute:** Transition
* **Presentation Order:** Team numbers do not determine presentation order.
